Radar

Privacy Policy

Effective 5 August 2026. This covers the Radar app on Android and the Radar web app.

Who is responsible

Radar is built and run by Michał Dakowicz, an individual developer based in Poland, who is the data controller for everything described here. Questions, requests and complaints all go to one place: michal.dakowicz2007@gmail.com.

The short version

Radar stores the library you build, the things you mark watched, and the account those belong to. There are no ads, no analytics SDKs, no trackers, and nothing is sold or shared for marketing. You choose whether anyone else can see your shelf. You can export everything as JSON at any time, and you can ask for the whole account to be deleted.

What Radar stores about you

Radar has no server of its own. Your account and its contents live in a Supabase project, in Postgres, under your user id.

What Why it exists
Email address, and a hashed password if you signed up with one To have an account you can sign back in to

Your profile picture is stored inside the database record itself, not in a separate public file store, so it is only visible to whoever is allowed to see your profile.

Signing in

You can sign in with an email address and password, or with Google. If you use Google, the sign-in happens in a browser window and Radar receives your email address and basic profile details from Google in order to create or match your account — Radar never sees your Google password. Authentication is handled by Supabase Auth; Radar itself never stores your password in readable form.

What Radar does not do

  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers
  • No analytics or crash-reporting SDK — there is no usage tracking in the app
  • No selling, renting or sharing of your data with anyone for their own purposes
  • No location tracking. Radar asks for a region only as a text setting, so it can show the right release dates and streaming services
  • No reading of your contacts, calendar, messages, call logs or files

Who else handles your data

These are the only third parties involved, and each is used for one job:

  • Supabase — hosts the database and handles authentication. Everything in the table above sits there. Their privacy policy.
  • Google Firebase Cloud Messaging — delivers push notifications to your device. It receives the device token and the notification itself. Firebase privacy.
  • Firebase Hosting — serves the Radar web app and this page. It keeps standard web request logs, including IP addresses.
  • Google Sign-In — only if you choose to sign in with Google.
  • The Movie Database (TMDB) — supplies posters, cast, runtimes and release dates. Radar asks TMDB about titles and ids. Your account, your email and your library are never sent to TMDB. TMDB privacy policy.

These providers operate infrastructure outside Poland, so your data may be stored and processed outside your own country, including in the United States. Radar relies on the providers' own transfer safeguards for this.

Radar uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB.

What other people can see

Settings → Profile visibility decides this, and it is enforced on the server rather than in the app, so changing it takes effect for everyone immediately:

  • Only Me — nobody else can see your library, activity or friends list
  • Friends — only accounts you have accepted as friends
  • Public — anyone with the link to your shelf, including people without a Radar account

Your username and display name stay searchable by other Radar users whatever you choose, so that friend requests are possible at all. Anything you write in a comment is visible to whoever can see that activity entry.

What stays on your device

Radar keeps your preferences locally — theme, card size, library filters and grouping, the stats period, which release notes you have seen. These never leave the device and go away when you uninstall the app.

If you set a profile picture, Radar asks for access to your photos so you can pick one, and the image is processed on the device before being saved to your profile. If you save a Recap card to your gallery, Radar asks for permission to write that one image. Radar does not read or upload the rest of your photo library.

How long it is kept, and how to get rid of it

  • Your account and its contents are kept for as long as the account exists. Signing out does not delete anything.
  • Export: Settings → Data has a JSON export of your whole library, so you can leave with your data at any time.
  • Deletion: email michal.dakowicz2007@gmail.com from the address on the account and ask for it to be deleted. The account, the library, the activity, the comments, the friendships, the notifications, the recaps and the device tokens are all removed within 30 days. Full steps and what is kept.
  • A few traces outlive the account briefly: database backups roll off within a further 30 days, the deletion request email is kept for up to 12 months as the record that it was honoured, and web server logs expire within 30 days.

Security

All traffic between the app and Supabase runs over HTTPS. Access to your rows is restricted at the database level by row-level security policies keyed to your user id, so one account cannot read another's library except through the visibility rules above. No system is perfect, and Radar is a solo project — if you find a problem, please write rather than publish it.

Your rights

Radar is run from Poland and follows the GDPR. You can ask for a copy of your data, ask for it to be corrected or deleted, ask for processing to be restricted, object to processing, or ask for it in a portable format — the JSON export covers the last one instantly. Where Radar relies on your consent, such as notifications, you can withdraw it in Settings or in the Android system settings at any time.

The legal bases are: performing the agreement to provide you the app, for your account and library; your consent, for notifications and for picking a profile picture; and legitimate interest, for keeping the service secure and working.

Write to michal.dakowicz2007@gmail.com to exercise any of these. If you are unhappy with the answer you can complain to the Polish supervisory authority, the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (UODO).

Children

Radar is not designed for children and is not directed at anyone under 13. If you believe a child has created an account, write and it will be removed.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes in a way that affects what is collected or who handles it, the effective date at the top changes and the change is noted in the app's release notes. Older versions are visible in the project's public repository history.
